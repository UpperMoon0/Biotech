# Multiblock redesign: existing-world policy

## 2.7 habitat entrance revision

New habitat construction is 7 × 7 × 5, with a two-block-wide, three-block-high doorway beside the controller and a level path into the rear shelter. The controller and seven ports retain their exact 2.6 coordinates and facings. The additional height raises the canopy above the walking area; it does not relocate existing blocks.

The complete 2.6 habitat is frozen in `design/terrestrial-habitat-2.6.json` and checked independently after the 2.7 pattern and before the 2.5 fallback. Existing 2.6 habitats continue operating with their original low opening until the player deliberately rebuilds them. Mixed partial layouts do not form. Finish the cycle and empty inventories/fluids before rebuilding, or construct a new habitat separately. Construction previews and the guide show 2.7 only.

The Service Pipe registry ID and geometry are unchanged; its block model now inherits vanilla block display transforms so inventory and held views reveal its cylindrical body and flanges. No item replacement or world migration is required.

## Existing builds remain valid

Every complete 2.5 structure is still accepted, with exactly its original controller origin, hatch coordinates, facings and block-state requirements. There is no automatic replacement, conversion or relocation of blocks. All controller and hatch registry IDs, block entity types, NBT fields, recipes, storage capacities and transaction code remain unchanged. No output rolls are regenerated by this update.

The validator first checks the new build, then the entire old build. It does not accept an arbitrary mixture of old/new parts. Only a successful complete check chooses the hatch layout. Invalid structures pause through the unchanged NsTut Lib transaction engine; selection is recomputed after loading and before processing. This supports unchanged saved inventories, fluids, FE, captured-animal stacks, consumed-input state, active snapshots, progress and saved output decisions.

New construction previews deliberately show the new layout. They do not overwrite or upgrade old machines. Smart Hammer and preview ownership remains entirely in NsTut Lib.

## Optional rebuilding

The safest upgrade is to leave existing production lines alone and build the redesigned machine separately. To replace a running machine deliberately:

1. Stop supplying new input and let its active cycle finish. Clear blocked output space first.
2. Pause the controller with its redstone setting. Empty every item/fluid hatch and recover stored energy through the supported automation/setup workflow where possible. Do not assume breaking a hatch preserves FE or fluids.
3. Only then dismantle/rebuild from the new preview and reconnect pipes to the marked facing sides.

Do not move or break a controller with an active cycle, or a loaded hatch, to follow the new preview. This update does not add an inventory-moving mechanism and does not promise content-preserving block drops. Simply installing the update requires none of these rebuilding steps.

## Design and provenance

The shared graphite/ivory/teal parts comprise structural frames, enamel shells, vent grilles, vessel walls, drives, grow lights, framed inspection glass and narrow service pipes. Farmland, water and grass remain because they represent growing/animal-care surfaces rather than manufactured parts. Controller on/off indicators and arrow/type-marked directional hatches keep their existing identities.

See [material counts and crafting budgets](machine-materials.md). Canonical new patterns live in `design/machines.json`; the generator checks exposed ports, origins and cardinal-direction source data and emits native Java and guide previews together. Existing exact legacy Java remains independently authored and unchanged.

Built-in image generation produced `art/biotech-material-atlas.png` and `art/biotech-glass-source.png`. The production assets in `common/src/main/resources/assets/biotech/textures/block/biotech_*.png` are cropped/downsampled 32×32 exports of those sources. The pipe model reuses vessel material. Prompts requested flat orthographic Minecraft-style pixel textures, consistent graphite steel/desaturated ivory enamel/dark teal trim, functional rivets/grilles/vessel bands/rotor/luminous bars, no text or perspective; the glass generation requested a genuinely transparent center and sparse cyan glints. These are generated texture assets, not screenshots.
