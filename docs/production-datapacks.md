# Livestock production datapacks

Slaughterhouse recipes with empty `itemOutputs` resolve each consumed animal's concrete loot table (including per-entity overrides and variant state) once. Retained catalysts never grant death loot. A dynamic recipe must consume at least one reconstructible living animal. Nonempty `itemOutputs` instead opt into static products: their authored counts and chances are kept, and the server multiplier does not apply.

`slaughterhouseYieldMultiplier` in the world/server Biotech server config is an integer from 1 to 64, default 2. It synchronizes to clients. It multiplies post-roll item counts, not probability; it provides no player or looting context. A captured death-loot seed is respected. In-flight results and donor identity are snapshotted, so config changes, retries and reloads affect only new transactions. Counts are split at the item's stack limit (also capped at 99 for persistence); tables producing more than 256 output entries are rejected before consumption, with a server warning. Reduce those tables or the multiplier to resume.

## Renewable extension example

Copy the appropriate `examples/production/<target>/goat_milk.json` to your datapack as `data/<your_namespace>/recipes/goat_milk.json` on Forge 1.20.1, or `data/<your_namespace>/recipe/goat_milk.json` on NeoForge. These examples are not installed as recipes by Biotech. Identical copies are packaged as inert `biotech_examples/goat_milk.json` resources for the codec/transaction regression tests.

The example keeps an adult `minecraft:goat`, consumes two wheat and 250 mB water per 32,000 FE cycle, and produces 1,000 mB `minecraft:milk` plus one manure. A steady 512 FE/t requires 63 processing ticks. For a modded species, replace `minecraft:goat` with its registered entity ID, add it to `#biotech:capturable`, and replace the products with registered item/fluid IDs. The mod providing those IDs must be installed. `EntityType` and `BiotechRecipeLifecycle` belong in the required input's tag (Forge) or `minecraft:custom_data` component (NeoForge); `isConsumable: false` keeps the selected adult. Capture eligibility alone does not create a production recipe.

The ordinary defaults use two/four food, 250/500 mB water and 32,000/64,000 FE, yielding one/three eggs or color-preserving wool, or 1,000/3,000 mB milk, with one/three manure. Power outages and full item or fluid outputs delay completion without rerolling products or consuming a retained animal. Both outputs must fit before resources are consumed.

Test-only loot tables under `biotech:gametest/` are unreferenced by ordinary recipes. They permit deterministic seeded probability, player-only exclusion and oversize safety tests without replacing vanilla loot tables.

## Greenhouse harvests and breeding

An empty Greenhouse `itemOutputs` list opts into mature-block loot. One consumable crop ingredient is required: vanilla seeds/produce, cactus, sugar cane, Nether wart, or a modded `CropBlock` item/seed. Melon and pumpkin seeds map to their fruit blocks. Crops use maximum age; cactus and sugar cane model three harvested blocks per plant. Ordinary blocks and block-entity plants are rejected before consumption. Other modded crops can use explicit nonempty outputs.

Each planted item contributes one independent mature harvest. Fertilizer adds half as many harvests, rounded down; default two-seed recipes therefore roll two harvests normally or three with fertilizer. Costs remain 400/500 mB water and 128000/160000 FE. This replaces the old invented fixed yields: mature wheat/beetroot give two main products per normal cycle, seed counts vary, and melons yield slices. Pack authors wanting the old balance should supply explicit item outputs. Harvest loot uses the machine position, mature block state and an empty tool, with no player, Fortune or Silk Touch. Drops are resolved once and persisted, so blocked outputs, pauses and block-entity reloads cannot reroll them. Loot output limits match Slaughterhouse's 256-entry snapshot contract.

JEI receives active server crop loot identities and 256 private-seed sample estimates on login/reload. Estimates describe ordinary harvest conditions; location-dependent tables can yield differently at the actual machine. Slots show approximate per-cycle counts and brief plain-language tooltips. Explicit output recipes retain authored quantities/chances.

Breeding requires exactly two reconstructible adult animals satisfying vanilla mating requirements. The default horse/donkey hybrid recipe produces a mule. It invokes that type's vanilla `getBreedOffspring` for each resolved newborn, using both parents. Sheep use the server's dye-mixing recipes; other animal variants and attributes follow their vanilla implementation. The newborn's own state is sanitized and set to age -24000. Retained parents remain unchanged. Chance groups and offspring are resolved once before transaction consumption and persisted across blocked ticks/reloads. Modded breeding implementations can return no child; those cycles fail safely without consuming inputs. Cross-species outputs require an explicit matching recipe; the default horse/donkey recipe preserves vanilla mule identity and inherited attributes.

## Default terrestrial animal coverage

The capture tag includes the five legacy livestock species plus horse, donkey, mule, skeleton horse, zombie horse, llama, trader llama, camel, goat, wolf, cat, ocelot, fox, panda, polar bear, parrot, bee, turtle, frog, sniffer, mooshroom, hoglin and strider. Armadillo is added in 1.21.1 and later. Happy ghast and camel husk are added in 26.1.2. These are vanilla non-aquatic `Animal` types, including land-associated flying animals and Nether animals. Allays, bats, golems and monsters outside the Animal hierarchy are not added; fully aquatic animals, including axolotls, are excluded. Datapacks can still opt in additional reconstructible types.

Every additional species has an adult Slaughterhouse selector (real loot can be empty) and a renewable Habitat manure recipe. Goats and mooshrooms also give milk. Where vanilla supports juvenile forms, Habitat raises one concrete captured juvenile to adulthood while retaining its full traits; turtles also give one scute. Species with no vanilla food require water/energy alone for these Habitat recipes. Other defaults consume their listed vanilla food. Production costs use the existing tier-one rates: 32,000 FE/250 mB water for renewable production, 48,000 FE/400 mB for growth, and 16,000 FE/200 mB for slaughter.

Breedable types have two retained, separate single-stack parents, two of their listed vanilla breeding food, 200 mB water and 20,000 FE. Horses/donkeys use golden carrots, llamas hay, camels cactus, goats/mooshrooms wheat, wolves beef, cats/ocelots cod, foxes sweet berries, pandas bamboo, bees dandelions, turtles seagrass, frogs slime balls, sniffers torchflower seeds, hoglins crimson fungus, striders warped fungus and armadillos spider eyes. Mating eligibility is delegated to vanilla, including taming and horse-family health conditions; the machine supplies the love/cooldown step on temporary reconstructed parents without modifying the retained items. Horse/donkey offspring are mules. Trader llamas produce ordinary llamas. Frogs produce frogspawn; turtles/sniffers produce eggs. Mules, skeleton/zombie horses, polar bears, parrots, happy ghasts and camel husks do not receive invented breeding recipes. Frogs, parrots and camel husks do not receive invented baby variants.

Captured-item traits preserve each individual's existing state. Newborn attributes, horse coats/markings, llama strength, panda genes and other vanilla inheritance come from both parents through vanilla offspring creation; growth preserves the same individual's traits. Tooltip health is expressed in hearts. Speed and jump strength show the effective saved Minecraft attribute values so animals of the same type can be compared. The tooltip does not expose raw NBT, owner UUIDs or world positions. Named defaults in the creative tab use factory-default traits; they do not silently tame animals.

The generator validates version-specific totals (156/160/165), common recipe parity and the permitted newer species, including the `scute` → `turtle_scute` item rename. Native tests check every default type against the loaded capture tag, reconstructed adult/juvenile creative entries, actual breeding foods and machine recipes. Horse regressions verify vanilla mixed speed/jump/health through recipe encoding and decoding, mule identity, retained parents and rejection of untamed breeding.
